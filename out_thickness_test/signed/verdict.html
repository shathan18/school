Signed damage — hypothesis test result

Headline: the trade-off is PARTIALLY SEPARABLE. Good and bad cross-talk do NOT move in lockstep.

Signed damage (credit=0.5) achieves 5× less bad cross-talk than the random control (23.1% → 4.6%) while simultaneously doubling good cross-talk (15.4% → 31.3%) — and it beats the control on every fidelity metric too. If the two were the same inseparable quantity, this point could not exist.

But a marginal frontier does exist — it is not free

Within the signed arms, more credit buys more good at the cost of more bad: credit 0.5 → 2.0 moves B_good 31.3% → 33.8% while B_bad climbs 4.6% → 9.0%. So there IS a real trade-off at the margin. What the experiment shows is that the random baseline sat far inside the frontier — it was paying a huge amount of bad cross-talk for very little good. The honest statement for the paper: not fundamental, but not free either.

The predicted double-counting is visible

Wall B fidelity degrades monotonically as credit rises (B_ssim 0.843 → 0.836 → 0.828; B_rmse 0.188 → 0.195 → 0.208). That is the over-darkening effect: an A-shard is credited for darkening a spot Wall B wants dark, but Wall B's own shards darken it too. Hence credit=0.5 is the sweet spot — the lowest weight tested already captures nearly all the joint-intersection gain without over-darkening. Reported because it was predicted in advance, not discovered by cherry-picking.

Bonus: the panel collapse was an artifact, and it is fixed

Unsigned used only 7.3/14 panels; signed uses 12.4/14. The collapse was a symptom of the one-sided objective: with no reward for double duty, shards had no reason to occupy panels serving both walls, so they clustered on the few harmless ones. Give them a reason and they spread back out. The softmax scatter fix may not be needed.

Three-point comparison for the paper

  • Random (rng.choice) — 23.1% bad / 15.4% good. Far inside the frontier.
  • Harm-only damage — 2.5% bad / 2.5% good. Best raw fidelity, but eliminates the joint-intersection: it solves noise by refusing to interact with the other wall at all.
  • Signed damage (credit=0.5) — 4.6% bad / 31.3% good. Slightly below harm-only on raw fidelity, but the only arm that delivers the concept: panels genuinely serving both walls, with the stray shadow steered onto regions that want it.
