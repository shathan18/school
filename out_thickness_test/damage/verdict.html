Cross-talk-aware shard assignment — it works

Replaced the rng.choice host-panel draw with an argmax over cover − λ·damage, where damage is the visual error the shard's unavoidable secondary shadow would inflict on the other wall. Panel placement is unchanged — only the assignment stage differs, so the effect is attributable to this alone.

Two real costs the metrics nearly hid

1. It killed the joint-intersection too. B_good fell 15.4% → 2.5%. The damage function only ever penalises harm; it never rewards help (damage is clamped ≥ 0). So the optimum it finds is "make the secondary shadow land off-wall or on a dark region" — i.e. avoid the other wall entirely. Panels have stopped serving both walls. This is exactly the predicted trade-off frontier: good and bad cross-talk are the same physical event, so driving one to zero drives the other with it.

2. Shards collapsed onto half the panels. The render labels show 7/14 and 6/14 panels actually holding material — the rest are empty. The hard argmax funnels every shard onto whichever panels have harmless secondary projections. That wastes half the structure and weakens the "cryptic cloud" (fewer depth planes ⇒ each panel is more readable as a picture, which is the opposite of the concept's intent). A softmax over the objective with a temperature knob would recover the scatter; not attempted yet.

Where this leaves the project

The noise problem is solved — but by moving to the "no cross-talk, high fidelity" end of the frontier, not by converting bad cross-talk into good. If genuine joint-intersection is the goal, the damage function needs to be signed: negative (a credit) when the shard's secondary shadow actually supplies darkness/colour the other image wants there. That would let the search seek helpful double-duty shards rather than merely dodging harmful ones. Currently it only knows how to avoid.
