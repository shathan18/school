Verdict — winner: wide-diversity + coarse shards (0.5×), seed 3

Trade-off statement: wide-angle diversity with coarse shards cuts Wall B bad cross-talk to 11.0% (from 32.7% — a 66% reduction, well under the 15% target) while preserving 14.4% genuine joint-intersection (good cross-talk, the highest of any passing config), at fidelity that actually beats tonight's best on both walls (Wall B SSIM 0.732→0.825, RMSE 0.253→0.207; Wall A 0.758→0.792). Passes both targets with margin.

The key insight — why "wide", not "near-axis": near-axis diversity also cuts bad cross-talk (to ~12-15%), but it does so by abandoning joint-intersection — its good cross-talk collapses to 5-9% (mean 7.1%), because axis-aligned panels each serve only one wall (that's the original baseline design). Wide diversity cuts the bad cross-talk while keeping the good (mean 13.2%). So you don't have to give up the joint-intersection effect to kill the noise — that's the real result.

Honest caveat — seed sensitivity

All four configs that pass BOTH targets are at seed 3. At seed 5 the same angle/density cells give B_bad 15-18% — narrowly OVER the 15% line. The angle trend (wide & near-axis ≪ moderate for bad cross-talk) holds across both seeds, and every config beats the 32.7% baseline substantially — but hitting <15% robustly needs picking a good seed. Practically: the winning setup is "wide/0.5× searched over a few seeds, keep the lowest-bad one," not a single deterministic config. I'm flagging this rather than quietly reporting only the seed-3 number.
