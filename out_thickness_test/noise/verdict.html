Noise-source diagnosis — apples/breakfast, seed 3 (color-fixed pipeline)

Two candidate noise sources isolated and measured independently. Verdict up front: the dominant visible noise is cross-talk (panels primary to one wall casting ghost shadows onto the other), not tiling density. Tiling density, if anything, improves the image as shard count rises.

1. Cross-talk (the "orphan/stray shard" source) — dominant

Decomposing each wall's rendered darkness by whether it comes from that wall's own (primary) panels or from cross-talk (non-primary) panels:

  • Wall A (apple): cross-talk = 16% of dark pixels, only 2.5% lands outside the subject → fairly clean.
  • Wall B (croissant/cup): cross-talk = 50.8% of dark pixels, and 32.7% of all dark pixels are cross-talk landing OUTSIDE the subject — those are the stray red marks (the red apple ghosting onto B). Own-wall misplacement ("type-a orphans") is negligible: 600 px vs 23,307 cross-talk px.

3. Verdict

Cross-talk is the dominant source of the visible noise, overwhelmingly on Wall B (a third of its rendered darkness is stray cross-talk outside the subject). Tiling density is not a noise source — it's a fidelity lever that helps as it rises. Type-a orphans (shards misplaced on their own wall) are negligible. The clearest evidence is the Wall B pair in the first figure: same layout, same seed, cross-talk removed → the stray marks disappear and SSIM jumps 0.73→0.86, edge-fidelity 0.25→0.47.

No fix proposed yet, per instruction — this is diagnosis only. Note the cross-talk removal shown here is a diagnostic upper bound, not a physical fix: a real panel casts on both walls, so reducing cross-talk means changing placement (or joint-optimizing it), not deleting shadows.
